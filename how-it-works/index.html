How colorgods works

colorgods builds tonal color scales from one color: your scale, a neutral scale and, if you want them, semantic palettes, all with the same contrast at each step. This page explains each part of the app and the method behind it.

Settings

Main color

Your color. Hex, RGB or HSL. Your color is kept exactly: it becomes one of the steps of your scale, unchanged.

Palette name. The name of your scale in the exports, as in color/blue/500 or --color-blue-500. We suggest one from the hue. Use lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens, starting with a letter; neutral and the semantic role names are taken.

Steps. How many colors each scale has: from 3 up to the maximum of the names you choose.

Names. Pick the names your design system already uses, so the exported tokens fit in without renaming.

  • Tailwind (50 to 950) and SDS (100 to 1000, from Figma's Simple Design System): each step is named after its position between the lightest and the darkest step, rounded to 50, so the names stay in order with any number of steps. Up to 16 steps with Tailwind and 19 with SDS, so no two steps share a name.
  • Radix: 1 to N, up to 30. You get the names, not the scale: your color sits on the step with the closest contrast, not necessarily step 9. For the Radix scale itself, use Radix Colors.
  • Custom: your own names, one per step, or filled with "1 to N" or "multiples of". If the number of names doesn't match the steps, the app says so and export waits until it does; it never changes your names for you.

Chroma curve. How saturated each step is, taken from one of the 17 color palettes of Tailwind CSS. We suggest the one closest to your hue. The curve doesn't change contrast: the lightness of each step comes from the contrast levels (see The method). The chart shows the chroma of each step.

Keep chroma at or below my color's. No step of your scale is more saturated than your color. Lightness and contrast don't change. It only affects your color's scale.

Neutral tint. How much of your hue goes into the neutral scale, from 0 (pure gray) to 1. Only the color of the neutrals changes, not their contrast.

Semantic palettes

Turn on danger, warning, success and info as you need them. Each role uses a Tailwind CSS palette with the hue design systems use for that role: we compared the error, warning, success and info colors of up to nine public design systems, such as Material 3 and Spectrum. By default, red, amber, green and blue. Each palette is placed on the same contrast levels as your scale, so it has the same steps, names and contrast; its hue and chroma are its own. The chroma curve and the chroma cap don't affect it, and your color only changes it in one case: if your color is too close to danger, warning or success, that role switches to another palette design systems also use for it (orange or yellow for warning, emerald for success). Danger has no alternative, so you get a warning instead. You can also choose another palette for the role, or type your own color: its scale is built like yours, with its suggested curve and without the cap.

Summary

The first row of the result sums up what was calculated: your color, steps, names, curve, tint and chroma cap. Copy link copies a link with all your settings, to share the result or come back to it.

Warnings and notes

Warnings point to something worth changing; notes explain something you may notice. Each one starts with the name of its scale.

  • Very light or very dark color: almost every step falls on one side of it, and the middle steps may come out very saturated.
  • No chroma: your color is gray, so its scale is gray and the neutrals have no tint.
  • Clip: a step doesn't fit in sRGB, so its chroma is reduced. The Clip column of the table says by how much.
  • Steps very close: two neighboring steps are closer in lightness than any two in the Tailwind CSS palettes. Fewer steps help.
  • Semantic palettes: a role is too close to your color or to another role, or its palette was changed for that reason.

Show in table opens the table of that scale on the step it names.

Scales

Every scale, one strip each. The same step has the same contrast in every scale, so you can swap one color for another of the same step without checking contrast again. ✓ marks your color, which keeps its own contrast.

The White AA and Black AA rows mark the steps where normal text meets WCAG AA (4.5:1) on white or on black, in every scale shown. The text above the strips also gives the steps for 3:1, enough for large text and interface components. Your color's step is left out of the rule and its contrast is given apart.

Contrast pair

Choose a text color and a background among all the steps, white and black. You see a sample, the ratio and which of the five WCAG levels it passes: normal text (AA 4.5, AAA 7), large text (AA 3, AAA 4.5) and interface components (3). The ratio is truncated, not rounded, so a pair that fails 4.5 never shows 4.50.

Tables

One table per scale, in tabs. Each row is a step: name, sample, oklch(), hex, contrast with white, contrast with black and clip, the share of chroma lost to fit in sRGB.

Export

Exports carry every scale: yours, the neutrals and each semantic palette turned on. Download the file or copy its content.

  • DTCG for Figma (Value.tokens.json, the Design Tokens format): in Figma, open the Variables panel and drag the file in. It creates a collection with one color variable per step, grouped as color/<palette>/<step>. The variables come hidden from publishing, so they don't reach your library until you decide.
  • CSS (colors.css): custom properties on :root, like --color-blue-500.
  • Tailwind @theme (theme.css), for Tailwind CSS 4: the same variables inside @theme. Each palette starts with --color-<palette>-*: initial;, so it replaces a Tailwind palette of the same name entirely instead of mixing with its steps (Disabling default colors).

If a setting has an error, Export lists it with a link to the field.

The method

Each step is a contrast level. At each step, every scale has the same relative luminance, the value WCAG uses to calculate contrast, so the same contrast with white and with black. The levels are the median of the 17 Tailwind CSS color palettes.

Your color keeps its exact value and replaces the step with the closest contrast; that step keeps your color's own contrast. The other steps of your scale take your hue, and the chroma of the curve scaled to your color's. The semantic palettes take the hue and chroma of their Tailwind CSS palette. The neutrals take the chroma of Tailwind's gray times the tint, with your hue. When a color doesn't fit in sRGB, its chroma is reduced (clip) and its contrast is kept.

The chroma curves come from Tailwind CSS (MIT license). colorgods grows from the "Generate scales" tool of DesignToken101. The code is on GitHub.
